Meeting people when social situations feel overwhelming

Reduce the size of the challenge: one place, one conversation, one repeat visit.

Updated 20 September 2026 · 9 minute read

When social situations create strong anxiety, “just go meet people” is not a useful instruction. A better approach is to make the situation more predictable and reduce the number of decisions you have to make in the moment.

Choose structured activities

Classes, volunteering, walking groups and board-game sessions give attention a shared target. That can feel easier than events where the only activity is mingling. Look for clear start/end times and descriptions of what happens when you arrive.

Create a tiny success definition

Your goal for the first visit might be: stay 30 minutes, ask one person one question, and learn whether you would return. You do not need to make a friend at the first event.

Prepare two opening lines

  • “Hi, is this your first time here too?”
  • “How did you find this group?”
  • “Do people usually come alone?”

Prepared lines reduce the cognitive load of inventing something while anxious.

Use repetition instead of intensity

Returning to the same group can be easier than attending many new events. Familiar faces reduce uncertainty, and small conversations can build naturally over several meetings.

Plan an exit before arriving

Know how you are getting home, where you can step outside, and what time you intend to leave. Having permission to leave can make staying feel more manageable.

Do not turn discomfort into a verdict

Feeling anxious does not automatically mean an event was wrong for you. Afterward, ask more specific questions: Was anyone welcoming? Did the structure help? Would a smaller group be easier? Did anxiety decrease after the first 15 minutes?

Important: Kindred provides general social guidance, not mental-health treatment. If anxiety is severe, persistent or significantly limits daily life, consider discussing it with a qualified health professional.
