HOST A GROUP

Start a small walking group

Walking gives people something to do while they talk, which can make a first meetup feel easier.

Updated 20 September 2026 · Practical planner included

A walking group can work well because it is inexpensive, flexible and easy to keep small. The key is to remove uncertainty: route, duration, meeting point, pace and what happens if the weather changes.

Build the first walk







Choose the meeting point carefully

Use a public, easy-to-identify place near transport or a known landmark. Avoid asking strangers to meet at a private home. Share the route area without publishing unnecessary personal details.

Make the group easier to join alone

Say explicitly that coming alone is normal. Newcomers often avoid events because they assume everyone else already knows each other. A sentence such as “Most people come independently; we will introduce everyone at the start” can reduce that barrier.

Set a simple hosting structure

  1. Arrive ten minutes early.
  2. Greet each person and introduce names.
  3. State the approximate duration and route.
  4. Begin with one easy group question.
  5. At the end, ask whether people want another walk rather than automatically adding everyone to a chat.

Weather and safety

Have a cancellation rule, avoid unsafe routes after dark, and consider accessibility. Participants should keep control over their own transport and personal information.
