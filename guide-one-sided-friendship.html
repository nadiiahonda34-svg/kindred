RECIPROCITY

When friendship starts to feel one-sided

Look for patterns over time, not one delayed reply or one difficult month.

Updated 20 September 2026 · 9 minute read

Reciprocity is broader than equal texting

People show care differently. One person may initiate more, while the other remembers details, makes time in person or offers practical support. The useful question is whether effort flows both ways in some form.

Patterns that deserve attention

  • You initiate almost every conversation and plan.
  • They repeatedly decline without suggesting alternatives.
  • They contact you mainly when they need something.
  • Your interests and problems receive little space.
  • You leave most interactions feeling used, anxious or invisible.

Run a small experiment instead of a confrontation

Reduce your initiating for a while and observe what happens. This can provide clearer information than repeatedly asking whether everything is okay.

If you want to address it directly

Use a specific observation rather than a character judgment: “I’ve noticed I’ve been initiating almost all our plans lately. I want the friendship to feel more mutual. How does it feel from your side?”

Allow the friendship to change level

Not every friendship needs to end. Some connections work better as occasional acquaintances rather than close friends. Adjusting expectations can protect your energy without creating unnecessary conflict.
