REMOTE WORK

Making friends when your workplace is your home

Remote work removes accidental contact, so social connection needs deliberate structure.

Updated 20 September 2026 · 8 minute read

Replace missing workplace repetition

Offices create repeated exposure automatically. Remote workers need to build that elsewhere. Choose a coworking day, regular café, class, sports session or volunteer shift that brings you around the same people.

Use the workday boundary

Schedule one social activity immediately after work once or twice a week. If you wait until you feel motivated later in the evening, it is easier to stay home.

Try “parallel social” spaces

Coworking, libraries and hobby workshops let you be around people without demanding constant interaction. They can be useful stepping stones if a networking event feels too performative.

Turn professional familiarity into personal connection carefully

If you meet another remote worker repeatedly, suggest a non-work activity: lunch, walk or shared local event. This tests whether the connection is broader than work talk.

Protect against isolation creep

Remote routines can gradually shrink without feeling dramatic. A weekly calendar check—“How many times will I leave home for something involving other people?”—can catch that early.
