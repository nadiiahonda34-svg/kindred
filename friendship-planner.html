Friendship Planner

Turn “we should do something sometime” into one simple plan you can actually send.

Updated 20 September 2026 · Works privately in your browser

Build a low-pressure plan

New friendships often stall because the next step stays vague. A useful invitation answers four things: what, when, how long and how easy it is to say no. Use the planner below to create something specific without making it feel heavy.







Why small plans work better

A first or second meetup does not need to prove that a friendship will last. Short plans reduce pressure and give both people useful information: Was conversation easy? Did both people contribute? Did the meeting leave you feeling comfortable rather than drained?

A simple follow-up rhythm

  1. Send one concrete invitation.
  2. If they cannot make it, notice whether they suggest another time.
  3. After a good meetup, follow up within a few days with something specific you discussed.
  4. If contact is always one-sided, reduce effort instead of repeatedly chasing.

Make it recurring

Friendship becomes easier when contact is attached to a rhythm: first Sunday coffee, Wednesday walk, monthly board-game night, or one voice note every weekend. Recurring plans remove the burden of constantly inventing a new reason to meet.

Keep the plan safe and flexible

For early meetings, choose a public place, keep independent transport, and avoid sharing exact home addresses too quickly. A good plan should be easy to shorten, change or leave.
